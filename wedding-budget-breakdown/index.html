Wedding Budget Breakdown

The standard percentage model wedding planners use — what share each category should get, how to adjust it to your priorities, and worked examples at three budget levels.

The standard percentage model

These shares come from industry surveys of thousands of real weddings. They add up to 100% and give you a sane starting point — not a rule carved in stone. Use our budget allocator to apply them to your own total instantly.

CategoryShareWhat it covers
Venue30%Ceremony & reception space, rentals, setup
Catering & bar23%Food, drinks, cake-cutting & service staff
Photography & videography12%Full-day coverage, albums, highlight film
Attire & beauty8%Dress/suit, alterations, hair & makeup
Flowers & decor8%Bouquets, centerpieces, ceremony florals, lighting
Music & entertainment7%Band or DJ, ceremony musicians
Cake & desserts3%Wedding cake, dessert table
Invitations & stationery3%Invites, save-the-dates, programs, signage
Transportation2%Couple's car, guest shuttles
Contingency fund4%Buffer for surprises — don't skip this

How to adjust the model to your priorities

  • Food-first couple? Move 3–5 points from decor into catering, or choose a venue that allows outside caterers.
  • Photos matter most? Push photography to 15% by trimming flowers and invitations — digital invites save real money.
  • Party people? A live band can push music to 10%+. Fund it from a simpler cake or a smaller floral package.
  • Small wedding? Fixed costs (photographer, attire) take a bigger share; per-guest costs (catering, rentals) shrink. Expect venue + catering to stay near 50% regardless.
  • Always keep contingency. Weather backups, overtime fees, and last-minute additions are the norm, not the exception.
Rule of thumb: decide your top two priorities first, fund those at the high end of their ranges, and let everything else sit at the low end. Couples who try to max out every category are the ones who blow the budget.

Common budgeting mistakes

  • Forgetting the "plus" costs: service charges, gratuities, and taxes can add 20–30% on top of a catering quote.
  • No contingency: the 4% buffer exists because nearly every wedding has at least one surprise expense.
  • Budgeting before the guest list: guest count drives catering, rentals, invitations, and favors — finalize it early.
  • Comparing packages without the fine print: "all-inclusive" means different things at different venues; always compare line by line.
